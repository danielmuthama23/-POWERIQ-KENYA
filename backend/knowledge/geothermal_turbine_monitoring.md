# Geothermal turbine condition monitoring

This note summarises widely used condition-monitoring practice. It is generic engineering guidance written for the POWERIQ prototype. Equipment-specific limits must come from the OEM manual and the operator's maintenance procedures; none are asserted here.

## Signals worth trending
Vibration (overall velocity or displacement at bearings), bearing metal temperature, lube-oil temperature and pressure, steam inlet pressure and flow, and generator winding temperature are the usual first-line indicators. Trending each signal against its own baseline at comparable load is more informative than comparing to a fixed alarm value.

## Typical degradation patterns
A slow rise in vibration with stable temperature often points to unbalance or alignment drift. Rising bearing temperature together with rising vibration suggests lubrication or bearing wear. A falling steam pressure ratio at constant flow can indicate scaling or erosion in the steam path. Sudden step changes usually indicate a sensor fault, a trip or a process change and should be cross-checked against other channels.

## Using a risk score
A model-based failure-risk score should prioritise inspection, not replace it. Review the contributing signals, the time since last maintenance and the fault history, and confirm on site before scheduling an outage.
